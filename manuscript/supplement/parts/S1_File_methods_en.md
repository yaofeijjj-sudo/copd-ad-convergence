# Supplementary Methods

This section gives the procedures and parameters that the Materials and methods section of the main article does not state. Each section continues the section of the same title in the main article. Numbered citations refer to the reference list of the main article, and Table A–M refer to the tables in S2 File.

### Co-expression networks and definition of Axis A

GSE76925 and GSE33000 were analyzed from the log-scale expression matrices supplied by the submitters. Outlying samples were those with standardized sample connectivity z < −2.5, and in the brain, age outliers within the AD and control groups were also removed by the 1.5 × interquartile range rule. Networks were built with deepSplit = 2 and a module merge height of 0.25. In the module–trait analyses, COPD and AD status were coded as case = 1 and control = 0 and sex as male = 1 and female = 0; GOLD stage was set to 0 when FEV₁/FVC ≥ 0.70 and otherwise to 1–4 by FEV₁ % predicted; emphysema burden on imaging was represented by the percentage of lung area with attenuation below −950 Hounsfield units (LAA-950) and by the lung density at the 15th percentile of the attenuation histogram (Perc15), with higher LAA-950 and lower Perc15 indicating greater burden; and Pi10 is the standardized airway wall thickness. The covariate-adjusted case–control models of the modules were BH-corrected within each organ. Module preservation was assessed with 1,000 permutations.

Only features that mapped unambiguously to a single current Entrez Gene ID were kept. The Fisher's exact tests of the 128 module pairs were BH-corrected. Functional enrichment used GO biological processes from org.Hs.eg.db and GO.db v3.22.0 and KEGG pathways retrieved with KEGGREST v1.50.0 on 25 June 2026. The over-representation analyses were one-sided against the 3,623-gene background, with BH correction within each axis and database, and the BH-adjusted P value of enrichment tests is denoted q.

### Replication in independent cohorts

The covariates of the disease-association model included, where available, smoking status, sequencing plate, post-mortem interval (PMI) and RNA integrity number (RIN). Analyses were restricted to samples with complete covariates (Table D in S2 File). In GSE47460, COPD was compared with controls with adjustment for age, sex and smoking status. The other analyses that use GSE47460 used the GPL14550 platform, which has more donors.

The GEO search was run on 3 October 2026 through E-utilities (db = gds) with the query alzheimer AND (frontal OR prefrontal OR BA9 OR BA10 OR BA46) AND Homo sapiens AND gse AND (expression profiling by array OR by high throughput sequencing), which returned 144 records. After removal of single-cell, sorted-cell, microdissected and non-AD records, the eligibility criteria given in the main article were applied, together with the requirement of a whole-tissue transcriptome. In GSE84422, cases classified neuropathologically as possible, probable or definite AD in the original study were combined as AD, and GSE313307 was additionally adjusted for sequencing plate and terminal respiratory infection. In GSE48350, controls were restricted to donors no younger than the youngest AD case, and GSM1176257, labeled AD, was removed because its raw CEL file was byte-identical to that of the control GSM350078 in GSE11882. The sensitivity analyses of the pooled effect were Hartung–Knapp inference, leaving out one dataset at a time, restricting cases to probable or definite AD, restricting to donors without terminal infection, and restricting to members measurable both on the GPL14550 platform of GSE47460 and in the six AD datasets (Table E in S2 File). At the member level, the standardized expression of each member was the outcome in the same model as the score, and the six AD datasets were pooled with the same random-effects model (sheet Fig3d in S1 Data).

The sigQC analysis used the expression matrices of both GSE47460 platforms and the six AD datasets, processed as in the replication analysis and including all samples. The primary metric was the median of the pairwise Spearman correlation matrix of Axis A members, compared with 1,000 random gene sets matched on mean expression (20 bins), with empirical P = (1 + number of random values ≥ observed value) / 1,001 (Table C in S2 File).

### Single-cell analyses

GSE302339 comprises 65 10x Genomics libraries from 32 COPD and 23 control donors and was analyzed from the filtered count matrices deposited in GEO. The GEO subject IDs cood8 and copd8 are libraries from two lobes of one donor and were analyzed as one donor. Quality control was performed within each library. Cells deviating by more than 3 median absolute deviations from the library median were removed with quickPerCellQC, testing total counts and detected genes on the log scale for low outliers and the mitochondrial percentage for high outliers, and doublets were then removed within each library with scDblFinder (Table F in S2 File). After normalization to 10,000 counts per cell and log1p transformation, each cell was annotated with the CellTypist Human_Lung_Atlas model in best-match mode, and the 60 resulting labels were grouped into six broad classes, namely myeloid, T/NK, B/plasma, epithelial, endothelial and stromal cells, and the class Other, which comprises 14 hematopoietic stem cells (Table F in S2 File). The UMAP embedding was rebuilt in scanpy v1.12.4 following the preprocessing in the original authors' public code, with library effects corrected by harmonypy v2.0.2. For the brain we used the SEA-AD release of 22 June 2026 with its published Subclass and Supertype annotations. UCell was run with maxRank = 1500. Scores were summarized as the donor mean for localization and as the donor median for disease association tests, and each donor × cell class combination contained at least 20 cells or nuclei. The between-class comparisons used fixed-effects models with donor-clustered CR2 variance computed with clubSandwich v0.7.0.

In the lung, COPD was compared with controls in myeloid cells and in alveolar macrophages, defined as cells with the CellTypist label Alveolar macrophages. In the brain, disease association analyses were restricted to nuclei profiled with 10x v3.1 chemistry that passed SEA-AD quality control. In immune supertypes with at least 30 eligible donors, the standardized DLPFC local continuous pseudoprogression score (CPS_Local) was the exposure, the linear models were adjusted for age, sex, PMI, RIN and study batch, and HC3 robust standard errors were estimated in statsmodels v0.14.6, with BH correction within each organ (Table F in S2 File). For pseudobulk analyses, raw UMI counts were summed within each donor and cell class (lung myeloid cells, 116,558 cells from 55 donors; lung alveolar macrophages, 64,542 cells from 55 donors; brain Microglia 2, 23,198 nuclei from 79 donors; all brain microglial supertypes, 79 donors) and processed with edgeR by filterByExpr filtering, trimmed mean of M values (TMM) normalization and dispersion estimation. fry was applied as a self-contained test, and CAMERA (inter.gene.cor = NA) was applied to the voom-transformed object as a competitive test. Lung models were adjusted for age, sex and smoking status; brain models used the standardized CPS_Local or SEA-AD AD neuropathologic change (intermediate or high vs not or low) as the exposure, both adjusted for age, sex, PMI, RIN and study batch.

### Cell proportion adjustment and comparison with myeloid signatures

Relative cell proportions were estimated with the CIBERSORTx web tool in October 2026. Single-cell references were sampled evenly by cell type across donors. The lung reference used the six major cell classes of GSE302339 with 300 cells per class from 55 donors; the brain reference used eight major classes of the SEA-AD DLPFC, namely excitatory neurons, inhibitory neurons, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, oligodendrocyte precursor cells, immune cells, endothelial cells, and vascular leptomeningeal and perivascular cells, with 250 nuclei per class from 80 donors. Signature matrices were built with the default web parameters; proportions were estimated in relative mode with S-mode batch correction, with quantile normalization for microarray data only, and 100 permutations. Log2-scale microarray expression was back-transformed to the linear scale, and RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data were supplied as counts per million. In the lung discovery cohort GSE76925, the proportion was added to the model adjusted for age, sex and smoking pack-years; the six AD datasets were estimated separately and pooled with the DerSimonian–Laird model. In GSE33000, the BRETIGEA microglial proportion was added to the covariate-adjusted model of the blue module eigengene (Table G in S2 File).

Myeloid expression specificity was computed from the CIBERSORTx single-cell reference as the mean counts per million of each gene in the target class divided by the sum of its class means. In each independent dataset, non-Axis A genes were grouped by mean expression within that dataset (10 quantile bins) and by myeloid specificity in the corresponding organ (5 quantile bins), and the 1,000 random gene sets were drawn according to the number of measurable Axis A members in each group; their disease effects were estimated with the same model as for Axis A, and the AD datasets were pooled within each draw with the DerSimonian–Laird model. Empirical P values were one-sided, given as the proportion of random-set effects greater than or equal to the Axis A effect. The BRETIGEA signature comprised the top 250 human microglial marker genes, and the GO gene sets were complement activation (GO:0006956) and phagocytosis (GO:0006909) (Table I in S2 File).

### Genetic analyses

Genes were located with NCBI build 37.3 coordinates, and SNPs were assigned to genes within gene boundaries after matching to the reference panel by rsID with the MAGMA synonym file. Default settings were used, with one-sided positive tests conditioned on gene size, gene density, inverse minor allele count and their logarithms; because per-SNP sample sizes vary in the AD summary statistics, per-gene sample size and its logarithm were also conditioned on by default. The *APOE* analysis removed all genes within 1 Mb of *APOE* with the gene-exclude option, the restricted background used the gene-include option with the 4,832 genes passing filters in both networks, and the conditional analyses entered the top 250 BRETIGEA human microglial marker genes or the Patir core signature as covariates with the condition option (Table J in S2 File). Both GWAS were of predominantly European ancestry. The AD summary statistics contributed 13,648,198 SNPs and 18,224 genes, with per-variant sample sizes of up to 147,103, and the COPD summary statistics 11,287,261 SNPs and 18,268 genes from 325,027 participants. Of the 137 Axis A genes, 130 were tested, and of the 434 Axis B genes, 409 (AD) and 410 (COPD) were tested.

### Peripheral blood and disease scope analyses

In GSE63060 and GSE63061, we used the AD and control samples that the submitters assigned to the AddNeuroMed case–control study, and the E-MTAB-5278 controls were current smokers. Among the microarray blood datasets, GSE54837 was log2-transformed and the others used the log2-scale matrices supplied by the submitters, all summarized to Entrez Gene; RNA-seq data (StringTie estimated counts for GSE248417) underwent low-expression filtering, edgeR TMM normalization and voom transformation. CAMERA was run with inter.gene.cor = NA and BH correction across the six datasets, and standardized scores were compared with the same design matrix (Table K in S2 File).

In GSE150910, the smoking comparison was between ever- and never-smokers without lung disease, and the psychiatric disorders in GSE53987 (Brodmann area 46 [BA46]) were schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and major depression. Comparator diseases were BH-corrected within the lung family, which comprised the IPF and ever-smoker comparisons in GSE150910, and within the brain family. On both GSE47460 platforms, IPF/UIP and other interstitial lung diseases were compared with controls, and COPD with IPF/UIP, using the same model, with unadjusted P values (Table D (b) in S2 File).

### Transcription factor analyses

Alveolar macrophages ranked second among lung myeloid subtypes by donor-mean Axis A score and covered all 55 donors; Microglia 2 ranked first among brain immune supertypes. The networks were built for alveolar macrophages from a fixed sample of 30,000 of 64,542 cells from 55 donors and for Microglia 2 from all 25,056 DLPFC nuclei from 80 donors, with patient and control cells combined. Networks were based on the hg38 promoter prior provided by CellOracle and included the top 3,000 highly variable genes, measurable Axis A members and detected transcription factors, fitted with bagging ridge regression. Of the 137 Axis A members, 136 were measurable in both models. Expression was normalized per cell and then natural-log transformed and imputed by k-nearest neighbors in CellOracle, and delta_X is the difference between simulated and imputed values on this scale. The simulated expression changes were averaged with equal weight across donors; genes predicted to increase in both models were counted as shared increases, and other non-zero changes as opposite-direction changes. The out-degree of a transcription factor is the number of its targets with non-zero network coefficients (Table L in S2 File).

Transcription factor activities were inferred with the univariate linear model of decoupleR (ulm, minsize = 5) on log counts per million of donor pseudobulk. CollecTRI was retrieved from OmniPath on 19 October 2026 and processed as in decoupleR get_collectri, with complexes merged into AP1 and NFKB. Activities of the ten top-ranked CellOracle transcription factors were tested with limma using the design matrices of the pseudobulk tests, with BH correction, and correlated with the donor Axis A score by Pearson correlation (Table M in S2 File).

### Statistical analysis

All steps involving random sampling used fixed random seeds, namely the expression-matched random gene sets of single-cell localization (lung 20260901, brain 20260831), the sigQC random gene sets (20261014), the random gene sets matched for both expression and myeloid specificity (20261019), and cell sampling and network fitting in CellOracle (123). Single-cell annotation, CellOracle and the donor-level pathology tests in the brain were run in Python v3.9.6, and all other analyses in R.
